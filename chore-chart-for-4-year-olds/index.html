Free chore chart for 4 year olds

Four year olds love a job that is properly theirs. A picture chart with three or four small jobs gives them a routine they can follow, with you checking in rather than doing it for them.

This age

What changes at four

Four is often the year before school starts, and children are building the independence they will need in Reception. They can follow a short routine, remember a job from yesterday and feel real pride in being trusted. They still need you nearby for anything with water, food or animals.

Ideas

Jobs a 4 year old can usually do

On their own

  • Put toys away
  • Clothes in the basket
  • Books back on the shelf
  • Shoes by the door
  • Pull the duvet up

With you nearby

  • Help lay the table
  • Water the plants
  • Help feed a pet
  • Match socks from the wash
  • Help unpack the shopping

A sample week

  • Every day: toys away, clothes in the basket, help lay the table, brush teeth
  • Once a week: water the plants or match the socks from the wash

Tips

Chores at four: what works

Give them a title

“Table helper” or “plant waterer” makes a small job feel important.

Same jobs every day

Repetition builds the habit. Keep the chart the same for a few weeks before you change it.

Let them tick

Holding the pen and ticking the box is half the fun at this age.

Keep it short

Four jobs at most. Finishing the chart should feel easy and happy.

Questions parents ask

What chores can a 4 year old do?

Putting toys away, clothes in the basket, helping to lay the table, watering plants and putting books back on the shelf.

How many chores should a 4 year old have?

Three or four small jobs a day, most of which take a minute or two.

Should I pay a 4 year old for chores?

Many parents don’t at this age. Praise, a sticker or a small privilege like choosing the bedtime story works well.
