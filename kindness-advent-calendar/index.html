Tips

Making it stick

Same time every day

Read the day’s act at breakfast, next to the chocolate calendar, so it becomes part of the morning.

Do it together

Younger children need a grown up for most acts. Doing it with them is the point.

Notice it out loud

“You made Grandad smile with that card” means more to a child than any sticker.

Finish with a treat

Pair it with the Christmas reward chart if you want a festive treat at the end.

Questions parents ask

What is a kindness advent calendar?

Instead of a chocolate behind each door, every day from 1 to 24 December has a small kind act or helping job, like making someone a card or helping lay the table. Your child colours in the star when it is done.

What age is it for?

The main list suits children of about 5 to 11. Choose the list for little ones for ages 3 to 5, with simpler acts like hanging a bauble or sharing a snack.

Can we use it with a normal advent calendar?

Yes. Lots of families do both: the chocolate in the morning and the kind act during the day. It turns December into a month of helping as well as counting down.

What if we miss a day?

Just do two the next day, or skip it. It is meant to be fun, so there is no catching up and no stars taken away.

Can I change the acts?

The printable has 24 ideas ready to go. If you want your own list every week, the free Score Your Chores app lets you add any quest you like and turns each one into points towards a reward you choose.

Score Your Chores is the same idea on your phone, for iPhone and Android. Kids wake up their own Buddy, finish quests with a press-and-hold, and save points for rewards you choose. You approve everything in two taps. Free, no ads, no child accounts.
