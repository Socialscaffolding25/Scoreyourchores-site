Free printable half-term chore chart

A week at home goes better with a little shape to it. Pick four or five jobs a day, agree a family treat for the end of half term, and let the chart do the reminding while everyone enjoys the break.

Tips

Making it stick

Agree the treat on day one

Pick something the whole family will enjoy at the end of the week, like a film night, a trip to the park or choosing a takeaway, and write it on the chart.

Keep the list short

It’s a holiday. Four or five small jobs a day is plenty, and mornings work best, before plans take over.

Mix jobs with fun

Reading, playing outside and helping make lunch count too, so the chart feels like part of the holiday rather than homework.

One chart per child

Make one for each child with their own name and Buddy, then count up the ticks together on the last day.

Questions parents ask

When is half term?

Most schools in England have a week off in late October, mid February and late May, but dates vary by area and by school, so check your school’s term dates.

What chores can kids do over half term?

Daily jobs like making the bed, tidying their room and laying the table, plus a few that suit a week at home: helping cook lunch, sorting toys they have outgrown, washing the car or helping in the garden.

How do I keep kids busy over half term without screens all day?

Give each day a simple shape: jobs in the morning, something active, then free time. Putting reading and playing outside on the chart makes the screen-free parts feel like part of the plan.
