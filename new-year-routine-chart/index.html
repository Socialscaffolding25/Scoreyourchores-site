Free printable New Year routine chart

After a fortnight of late nights and Christmas chocolate, the first week back at school can be a shock. A fresh chart on the wall gives the new year a clear start. Pick the steps that fit your family, add your child’s name and print it.

Tips

A good start to the year

Start before term does

Two or three days before school goes back, bring bedtime forward by 15 minutes a night and put the chart up. The first Monday is much calmer.

One new habit, not five

Add one new step for January, such as packing the bag the night before. Keep everything else familiar.

Let them choose the Buddy

Children stick with a chart they helped make. Let them pick the Buddy, the colour and the order of the steps.

Look back on Sunday

Count the ticks together at the end of the week and say what went well before you talk about anything else.

Questions parents ask

How do I get children back into a routine after Christmas?

Start a few days before school goes back. Bring bedtime forward by 15 minutes a night, bring back the usual morning steps and put the chart where everyone can see it.

Should children make New Year’s resolutions?

Keep it to one or two small habits they can do every day, such as reading for ten minutes or making their bed. A chart turns a resolution into something they can tick.

How many steps should a routine chart have?

Five to eight steps in the order they really happen. Fewer is better for under-fives.

What age is this chart for?

Children from about three can follow the picture steps with help, and most six to ten year olds can use it on their own.
