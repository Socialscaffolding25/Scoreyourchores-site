Chores for 3 to 5 year olds

Little ones love to help, and this is the age to make helping feel normal. Keep jobs short, physical and easy to see finished. Pictures matter more than words, so every chore on the chart below has an icon a pre-reader can recognise.

Tips

Making chores work at 3 to 5

Do it with them first

Show the chore, then do it together, then let them do it while you watch. Most three year olds can put toys away alone after a week of doing it side by side.

Keep it to three or four

A short chart they can finish beats a long one they give up on. Add a new chore only when the old ones happen without reminders.

Praise the effort, not the result

The duvet will be lumpy. Say “you made your bed all by yourself” and leave it. Redoing it teaches them their help doesn’t count.

Stickers work brilliantly

At this age a sticker is the reward. Let them choose the sticker and put it on the chart themselves.

Questions parents ask

What chores can a 3 year old do?

Three year olds can put toys in a box, put dirty clothes in the basket, carry their plate to the side, put shoes by the door and help feed a pet with you. Keep each job to a minute or two.

Should I reward a 4 year old for chores?

A sticker or a tick on a chart is plenty at four. The point is to make helping visible and fun. Bigger rewards can come later, when they are saving up points for something.

How many chores should a 5 year old have?

Three to five small daily chores is plenty. Choose a couple for the morning (get dressed, brush teeth) and a couple for the evening (toys away, clothes in the basket).
