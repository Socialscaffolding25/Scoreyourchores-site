Free printable potty training chart

A sticker for every try on the potty, a Buddy cheering them on and a small reward at the end. Add your toddler’s name, choose how many stickers it takes, and print it for the bathroom wall.

Tips

Making it work

Sticker for trying

Give a sticker for sitting on the potty and trying, not only when something happens. NHS advice is to praise your child for trying, whether or not they manage a wee or poo.

Keep the first reward close

Ten stickers means the first reward arrives within days. Move up to 15 or 20 once they have had a win.

Stay calm about accidents

Accidents are a normal part of learning. Clean up together, move on, and never take a sticker away.

Small, quick rewards

A new book, a trip to the park or choosing the bath toy. Deliver it the same day the chart is full.

Questions parents ask

When should I start potty training?

The NHS says almost all children can be potty trained between 18 months and two and a half years old, and that you don’t need to wait for your child to show signs or ask to use a potty before you start.

Do sticker charts help with potty training?

NHS advice suggests praising your child for trying and says you could give them a sticker or use a reward chart. Stickers for trying work better than stickers only for success.

What should I do about accidents?

Accidents happen to most children while they learn. Try not to get angry or upset: stay calm, clean up together and move on.

How many stickers should a potty chart have?

Start with 10 so the first reward comes quickly, then make the next chart a little longer.
