Free chore chart for 8 year olds

Eight year olds can take real responsibility for a few household jobs. A weekly chart with a few daily jobs and one weekly family job, plus a small reward for a full week, keeps the routine going without daily reminders.

This age

What changes at eight

At eight, children can plan ahead a little and enjoy being trusted with something that is always theirs. They can manage the same jobs for weeks without losing interest, as long as they can see progress. It is a good age to start linking a full week to a reward.

Ideas

Jobs a 8 year old can usually do

On their own

  • Make their bed
  • Tidy their bedroom
  • Clear the table
  • Put the recycling out on bin day
  • Dust low shelves

With you nearby

  • Help with the food shop
  • Help cook a simple meal
  • Clean out a pet’s bowl
  • Wash the car
  • Fold towels

A sample week

  • Every day: make your bed, tidy your room, clear the table
  • Once a week: recycling on bin day, dust the low shelves on Saturday

Tips

Chores at eight: what works

Give them ownership

A job that is always theirs, like the recycling, builds responsibility.

Weekly, not daily rewards

Eight year olds can work towards Sunday. Count the ticks together.

Raise the standard slowly

A tidy room means floor clear, bed made. Add the desk next month.

Notice without being asked

“You did the recycling without me saying” is the praise that sticks.

Questions parents ask

What chores can an 8 year old do?

Tidying their bedroom, clearing the table, sorting the recycling, dusting low shelves, helping with the shopping and simple pet care.

How many chores should an 8 year old have?

Three or four a day, or two daily jobs and one or two weekly ones.

Is a chore chart good for an 8 year old?

Yes. At eight, children like seeing their progress, and a chart turns reminders into something they check themselves.
