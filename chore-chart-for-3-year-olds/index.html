Free chore chart for 3 year olds

At three, a chore chart is less about getting jobs done and more about feeling like a helper. Pick three or four picture jobs your child can do with you nearby, add their name and print it for the fridge.

This age

What changes at three

Three year olds want to copy everything you do, which makes this the easiest age to start. They can’t read, they forget quickly and they tire fast, so the chart is a picture reminder for you both. Keep every job to a minute or two, and treat the tick as a celebration, not a test.

Ideas

Jobs a 3 year old can usually do

On their own

  • Put toys in a box
  • Clothes in the laundry basket
  • Shoes by the door
  • Books back on a low shelf

With you nearby

  • Brush teeth
  • Carry their plate to the side
  • Help feed a pet
  • Wipe up a small spill
  • Water a plant with a small jug

A sample week

  • Every day: toys in the box, clothes in the basket, shoes by the door, brush teeth with help
  • Once a week: help water the plants on Saturday

Tips

Chores at three: what works

Do it together

Three year olds learn by copying. Tidy the toys alongside them and let them finish the last few.

Pictures, not words

They can’t read the chart yet, so point to each picture and say the job out loud.

Tick straight away

A tick or sticker the moment the job is done means far more than one at bedtime.

Expect it to be wobbly

Socks in the basket count even if one misses. Praise the effort, not the result.

Questions parents ask

What chores can a 3 year old do?

Simple helper jobs: putting toys in a box, clothes in the laundry basket, shoes by the door, wiping up a small spill and helping to feed a pet.

How many chores should a 3 year old have?

Three or four short jobs a day is plenty, done with a grown-up close by.

Do 3 year olds understand reward charts?

Many do from around three, if the reward comes quickly. A sticker straight after the job works better than a prize at the end of the week.
