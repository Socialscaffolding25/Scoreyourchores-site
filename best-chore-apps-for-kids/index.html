Chore apps for kids: how to choose

There are dozens of chore apps, and they solve different problems. Here is what actually matters when you pick one for your family, the main types of app, and where ours fits. We make Score Your Chores, so we have tried to be fair about when a different kind of app is the better choice.

What to look for

Seven questions to ask

One shared phone, or a device for every child?

Younger children rarely have their own phone. An app that works on one family phone, with a card per child, is simpler and keeps children off personal devices.

Does a grown-up check the job?

Points that land automatically teach children to tap, not to tidy. Look for quick parent approval, ideally with a way to send a job back for one more go.

Rewards or real money?

Some apps are built around a bank card and money transfers. Others use points for rewards you choose. Neither is wrong, but they suit different ages.

Ads, tracking and prices aimed at children

Check whether children ever see adverts or purchase buttons. In a family app they shouldn’t.

Does it grow with your children?

Picture quests for a four year old and weekly responsibilities for a twelve year old need different setups.

Is the free plan actually useful?

Try it for a week before paying. A good app is useful from day one without a subscription.

Will they still want it in week three?

Novelty wears off. Streaks, collections and a weekly family goal are what keep children coming back.

The main types

Four kinds of chore app

Reward chart apps

Children earn points or stars for chores and swap them for rewards the parents choose. Best for ages 3 to 12 and families who want motivation without money changing hands.

Pocket money card apps

Chores are linked to real money on a prepaid card for the child, usually with a monthly subscription. Best for older children learning to spend and save real money.

Family organisers

Shared calendars, shopping lists and to-do lists for the grown-ups. Useful for running the house, but not built to motivate children.

Cleaning schedulers

Rotas for adult housework, room by room. Great for keeping the house on track, less so for getting a seven year old to feed the cat.

Questions parents ask

What is the best chore app for kids?

It depends on what you want it to do. For a reward chart that children enjoy and parents approve, pick an app built around points and rewards. If you want your child to have a bank card, a pocket money card app is the better fit.

Is there a free chore app for kids?

Yes. Score Your Chores is free to start, with no ads, and the free plan covers two children with every core feature. An optional subscription adds more children and extras.

Do kids need their own phone for a chore app?

Not with every app. Score Your Chores runs on one shared iPhone: each child taps their own card, and a grown-up PIN protects approvals and settings.

Should a chore app pay real money?

It doesn’t have to. Many families use points for privileges like screen time or choosing dinner, and hand over any pocket money themselves.
