A Kindness Buddy for December

A shelf friend that only notices the good. Print a stand-up paper Buddy, its letter to your child and 24 morning notes: some tell your child something kind the Buddy spotted, some give them a gentle mission. No naughty list, no telling tales, just a month of being noticed for good things.

How it works

Five minutes on 30 November

1. Print and cut

Four A4 pages. Card or thicker paper helps the Buddy stand up on its own.

2. The Buddy arrives

On 1 December, stand it on a shelf with its letter. It explains it only looks for good things.

3. A note each morning

Leave the next note by the Buddy each night. On “I spotted you” notes, write one real kind thing from that day.

4. Notice out loud

Read the note together at breakfast. Being seen for the good is what makes it work.

Why no naughty list?

Children try hardest when they feel noticed for what they get right. A December friend that watches for mistakes can make the month anxious; one that only spots kindness turns it into a game of being good to each other. If you like the idea of an elf on the shelf but not the naughty list, this is for you.

Questions parents ask

What is a Kindness Buddy?

A paper Buddy that arrives on 1 December and stays on a shelf until Christmas Eve. Each morning it leaves a little note: either something kind it spotted your child doing, or a gentle mission for the day.

Is it an alternative to the elf on the shelf?

It is for families who like the idea of a December shelf friend but not the naughty list. The Kindness Buddy never reports back and never threatens anything. It only notices the good.

What do I need?

A printer, scissors and five minutes. The kit prints on four A4 pages: the stand-up Buddy, its letter to your child and 24 notes. Card or thicker paper helps the Buddy stand up.

What do I write on the “I spotted you” notes?

Something real you saw that day, however small: helping carry the shopping, being gentle with the cat, trying hard at reading. Specific beats general, so “helping Grandad with his coat” lands better than “being good”.

What age is it for?

Children of about 3 to 9. Read the notes aloud for younger ones. Older children often like writing a note back to their Buddy.
