Tips

Making it work

One goal at a time

“Getting dressed on your own” is clearer than “being good”. Children need to know what earns a star.

Stars stay earned

Never take a star away. If stars can be lost, the chart stops feeling fair and children give up on it.

Keep the first reward close

Ten to fifteen stars means the first reward arrives within a week or two. Make the next chart a little longer.

Rewards that aren’t sweets

A film night, choosing dinner or an extra bedtime story. Time together often works better than things.

Questions parents ask

How does a star chart work?

Choose one behaviour to focus on, give a star each time it happens and agree a reward for a full chart before you start.

How many stars before a reward?

About 10 for under-fives and 15 to 20 for older children. The first reward should come quickly so they see the chart works.

Should you take stars away for bad behaviour?

No. Keep the chart for good things only and deal with bad behaviour separately, so earned stars always stay earned.

What is the difference between a star chart and a sticker chart?

Nothing much: both reward good choices. A printed star chart just needs a pen, so you never run out of stickers.
