Free chore chart for 12 year olds

Twelve year olds can take on grown-up jobs, like their own laundry or helping cook dinner. A chore chart at this age is about building independence, so let them help choose the jobs.

This age

What changes at twelve

By twelve, children are settling into secondary school and want to be treated as older. A chart works if it looks like their plan rather than a sticker chart, so let them pick the jobs, the colours and the order. Hand over whole jobs, start to finish, and step back.

Ideas

Jobs a 12 year old can usually do

On their own

  • Wash up
  • Put their laundry away
  • Change their bedsheets
  • Clean the bathroom sink
  • Sweep and tidy the kitchen

With you nearby

  • Help cook dinner using the hob
  • Run a wash with you
  • Hoover the house
  • Help a younger sibling, with a grown-up at home
  • Wash the car

A sample week

  • Every day: wash up, put your laundry away, help cook dinner
  • Once a week: change your sheets on Sunday

Tips

Chores at twelve: what works

Hand over a whole job

Putting their own laundry away, start to finish, is a great step at twelve.

Cook together once a week

A simple meal they plan and make, with you in the house for the hob and oven, builds confidence fast.

Respect their time

Agree when jobs are due, not exactly when they must be done.

Recognise maturity

Helping a younger sibling, with a grown-up at home, counts and deserves to be noticed.

Questions parents ask

What chores can a 12 year old do?

Washing up, putting their laundry away, changing their bedsheets, cleaning the kitchen, hoovering and cooking a simple meal with a grown-up nearby.

How many chores should a 12 year old have?

Two or three daily jobs, with a couple of bigger weekly jobs they own completely.

Is a chore chart too young for a 12 year old?

Not if they help design it. A clean, simple chart with their own jobs reads as a plan, not a sticker chart.
