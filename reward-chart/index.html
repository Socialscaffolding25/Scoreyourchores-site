Reward charts that work

Pick one thing to work on

Staying in bed, getting dressed without fuss, trying new food. One goal at a time is far easier for a child to follow.

Make the first reward quick

For young children, use 10 stickers so the first win comes within a week. Increase it once they have tasted success.

Never take stickers away

If it didn’t happen today, there is simply no sticker. Progress they have earned stays earned.

Celebrate the finish

Make a fuss when the chart is full, deliver the reward quickly, and start a new chart with a new goal.

Questions parents ask

How do reward charts work?

Your child earns a sticker or colours a circle each time they do the thing you are working on. When the chart is full, they get the reward you agreed at the start.

How many squares should a reward chart have?

Ten works well for children under five, so the reward comes within a week or two. Older children can manage 20 or 30 squares for a bigger reward.

What rewards work best?

Experiences and privileges usually work better than toys: choosing a family film, a trip to the park, baking together, staying up 20 minutes later or picking Friday’s dinner.

Can a reward chart be taken away?

Try not to remove stickers once earned. Taking progress away makes the chart feel unfair and children stop trying. Simply don’t add a sticker that day.
