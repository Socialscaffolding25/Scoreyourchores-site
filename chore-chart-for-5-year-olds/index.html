Free chore chart for 5 year olds

Starting school is a big change, and a simple chore chart helps five year olds feel capable at home too. Choose three or four jobs, including a couple of school morning steps, and print it for the bedroom door.

This age

What changes at five

Most five year olds are in Reception, where they hang up their coat, find their peg and tidy away without being asked. Bring the same expectations home. They can manage a short morning routine and a couple of jobs after school, and they can wait a few days for a reward.

Ideas

Jobs a 5 year old can usually do

On their own

  • Get dressed
  • Put toys away
  • Clothes in the basket
  • Shoes and coat on the peg
  • Pull the duvet up

With you nearby

  • Help lay the table
  • Feed a pet
  • Match socks
  • Water the plants
  • Help put the shopping away

A sample week

  • Every day: get dressed, toys away, help lay the table, brush teeth
  • Once a week: match the socks from the wash on Sunday

Tips

Chores at five: what works

Link it to school mornings

Getting dressed and shoes on can sit on the same chart as tidying up.

Show, then step back

Do a job together twice, then let them try alone while you watch.

A weekly mini reward

Five year olds can wait a few days. A Friday treat for a good week works well.

Celebrate firsts

The first time they make their bed alone deserves a fuss.

Questions parents ask

What chores can a 5 year old do?

Getting dressed, tidying toys, helping to lay the table, feeding a pet with you, matching socks and pulling the duvet up.

How many chores should a 5 year old have?

Three or four a day, including a couple of self-care jobs like getting dressed and brushing teeth.

How do I get my 5 year old to do chores?

Keep the jobs small, do them at the same time each day, and let them tick the chart themselves.
