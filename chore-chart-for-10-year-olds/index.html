Free chore chart for 10 year olds

Ten year olds can run part of the household on their own. A chart with two or three quick daily jobs and a weekly one or two gets them ready for secondary school, where nobody packs their bag for them.

This age

What changes at ten

Ten is the last full year before thinking about secondary school, and independence suddenly matters. Ten year olds can follow a weekly plan, judge when a job is done properly and remember without a prompt most days. They respond best when they are treated as part of the household team.

Ideas

Jobs a 10 year old can usually do

On their own

  • Load the dishwasher (sharp knives left for a grown-up)
  • Make their packed lunch
  • Hoover a room
  • Fold and put away laundry
  • Keep their room tidy

With you nearby

  • Help cook dinner
  • Clean the bathroom sink with a mild cleaner
  • Change their bedsheets
  • Wash the car
  • Walk the dog on a familiar route

A sample week

  • Every day: load the dishwasher, make your packed lunch, homework done
  • Once a week: hoover on Saturday, fold your laundry on Sunday

Tips

Chores at ten: what works

Mix daily and weekly

Packed lunch every school day, hoovering once a week.

Let them own the standard

Agree what “done” looks like together, then trust them to meet it.

Prepare for secondary

Packing their own bag and lunch now saves stress at eleven.

Make it a team effort

Ten year olds respond well to being treated as part of the household team.

Questions parents ask

What chores can a 10 year old do?

Loading the dishwasher, hoovering, folding laundry, making their packed lunch, helping cook dinner and cleaning the bathroom sink with a mild cleaner.

How many chores should a 10 year old have?

Two or three quick daily jobs, plus one or two bigger weekly ones.

Should a 10 year old be paid for chores?

Some families pay a weekly amount for a full chart at ten. Others keep chores unpaid and pay for extra jobs. Pick one and stick to it.
