Free printable Christmas chore chart

Turn December helping into a game. Festive jobs like decorating, writing cards and setting the table for visitors, with your child’s name and their own Buddy. Print it and put it next to the advent calendar.

Tips

Keeping December calm

Start on the 1st

Run it alongside the advent calendar so the chart becomes part of the countdown.

Small festive rewards

Choosing the Christmas film, hot chocolate by the tree or putting the star on top. Keep presents out of it.

Give to others

Add one kind job: sorting old toys for a charity shop or making a card for a neighbour.

Keep it short

Four or five jobs is plenty in a busy month. Swap one after the school holidays start.

Questions parents ask

When should we start a Christmas chore chart?

Start on 1 December so it runs alongside the advent calendar, or the first day of the school holidays if December is already busy.

What festive chores can kids do?

Helping decorate, writing cards and thank-you notes, wrapping, setting the table for visitors, sorting old toys for charity and helping with the baking.

Should Christmas chores earn presents?

We’d keep presents out of it. Small festive rewards work better: choosing the Christmas film, an extra story, hot chocolate by the tree or putting the star on top.
