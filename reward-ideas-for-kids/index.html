Guide · Reward ideas

54 reward ideas for kids that aren’t sweets or money

The best rewards cost little and mean a lot: your time, a choice that’s usually yours, or a small privilege. Here are ideas to put at the end of a chore chart or reward chart.

Time together

  • A one-to-one trip to the park
  • Baking something together
  • A board game of their choice
  • A bike ride together
  • Building a den in the living room
  • Ten minutes of your undivided attention
  • A walk to feed the ducks
  • Teaching you something they know
  • A picnic, even indoors
  • An extra bedtime story

Choices

  • Choosing Friday’s dinner
  • Choosing the family film
  • Picking the music in the car
  • Choosing the weekend activity
  • Choosing tomorrow’s pudding
  • Picking where to go for a walk
  • Choosing the bath bubbles
  • Choosing what goes in the packed lunch

Privileges

  • Staying up 20 minutes later
  • Extra screen time
  • Sitting in the front seat (if old enough)
  • A lie-in at the weekend
  • Skipping one chore
  • Having a friend round
  • Being ‘boss’ of family games night
  • Sleeping in a den or sleeping bag
  • Using the ‘grown-up’ plates
  • Having pudding first

Outings

  • A trip to the library
  • A swim
  • A visit to the soft play
  • A trip to the skate park
  • A day at the beach
  • Going to the cinema
  • A trip to a farm
  • Climbing wall or trampoline park
  • Visiting grandparents
  • A bus or train ride just for fun

Small things (not sweets)

  • A new book
  • Stickers
  • A comic or magazine
  • Seeds to grow
  • A new pen or pencil set
  • Bath toys
  • A small Lego set
  • Hair accessories
  • A new football
  • Craft supplies

Bigger goals to save up for

  • A day out of their choice
  • A new game
  • Tickets to a match or show
  • A sleepover party
  • A new bike accessory
  • A family pizza and film night

Questions parents ask

What are good rewards for kids that aren’t food?

Time together, choices and privileges work best: choosing the film or dinner, staying up a little later, a trip to the park, a board game with you or a friend round to play.

Should rewards be big or small?

Mostly small and often. Young children need a reward within days, not weeks. Save one bigger goal for older children who can wait and save points towards it.

Is it bribery to reward chores?

A bribe is offered in the moment to stop bad behaviour. A reward is agreed in advance for effort. Agreeing it together, and delivering it quickly, keeps it a reward.

Should screen time be a reward?

It can be, if screen time is limited anyway. Many families find 15 or 20 extra minutes is one of the most popular rewards on the list.
