How it works

How a shared phone chore app works

In Score Your Chores, the phone opens on a Who’s up screen with a card for each child. A child taps their card, sees their quests as pictures and words, and finishes each one with a press-and-hold. Their Buddy grows as they go.

Everything a grown-up controls, approvals, rewards and settings, sits behind a grown-up PIN. Children never see prices or buy buttons, there are no adverts, and children don’t need an email address or an account. It works the same on iPhone and Android.

What to look for

A lock on the grown-up area

Approvals, rewards and settings should need a PIN so children can’t approve their own jobs.

Quick switching between children

Siblings should be able to hand the phone over and find their own card in one tap.

Nothing to buy on the child’s side

No prices, adverts or purchase buttons anywhere a child can reach.

Icons let three to five year olds follow their list without help.

Set it up

Lock the family phone to one app

iPhone: turn on Guided Access

Go to Settings, then Accessibility, then Guided Access. Switch it on and set a Guided Access passcode that only grown-ups know.

iPhone: start a session

Open the chore app, triple-click the side button (or the Home button on older iPhones) and tap Start. The child can only use that app until you triple-click and enter the passcode.

Android: turn on App pinning

Open Settings and search for App pinning (on some phones it is called Pin windows or Screen pinning). Switch it on and choose to ask for your PIN before unpinning.

Android: pin the app

Open the chore app, go to the recent apps view, tap the app’s icon at the top of its card and choose Pin. To unpin, follow the on-screen steps and enter your PIN.

Keep notifications quiet

Turn on a Focus or Do Not Disturb mode while children have the phone, so messages and emails stay private.

Questions parents ask

Do children need their own phone for a chore app?

No. Some chore apps are built for one shared family phone. In Score Your Chores each child taps their own card on the family phone and there are no child accounts.

Can siblings share one phone for a chore app?

Yes. A shared phone chore app keeps a separate card, quests and points for each child, so siblings just tap their own name.

How do I stop children changing settings on a shared phone?

Choose an app with a grown-up PIN for approvals, rewards and settings. On iPhone, Guided Access can also keep a child inside one app, and on Android, App pinning does the same.

Which chore apps work without child accounts?

Score Your Chores and FamilyQuest both work without child accounts. Many other chore apps ask each child to log in on their own device.
